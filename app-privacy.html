Privacy Policy

Last updated: 16 November 2026

Luca Oropallo built the Rolify app as a Free app with optional in-app purchases. This SERVICE is provided by Luca Oropallo and is intended for use as is.

This page informs you about how the app handles information. If you choose to use the Service, you agree to the collection and use of information as described here. I will not use or share your information with anyone except as described in this Privacy Policy.

The short version

  • Rolify has no account. You never give me your name or email.
  • Your audio files, covers, playlists and adventures stay on your device and are never uploaded.
  • The services below receive anonymous identifiers that identify the app installation, not you.
  • Ad measurement (Meta and TikTok) only runs if you allow it where the law requires, and you can switch it off at any time in Menu > Privacy.

What stays on your device

Audio you import, sounds you download, cover images you pick, playlists, adventures and settings are stored only on your device. I cannot see them.

Information Collection and Use

The app uses third party services that may collect information used to identify you or your device. Each one acts for me as a service provider. Links to their privacy policies are listed at the end of this section.

Purchases (RevenueCat, Apple, Google)

Purchases are handled by RevenueCat together with the App Store or Google Play. RevenueCat creates a random, anonymous App User ID for the installation and records which products it owns. I do not link it to your name, email or any account. Apple and Google process the payment and never share your payment details with me. Purchases belong to your Apple or Google account, and “Restore Purchases” asks the store to give them back to the app.

Sound pack downloads (my server)

Sound packs come from a small server I run on Cloudflare. When you open the Shop or download a pack, the app sends the pack you want and the anonymous App User ID. The server asks RevenueCat whether that ID owns the pack and, if so, returns a short-lived download link. The server does not store the ID or who owns what. Like any web server, Cloudflare keeps technical request logs (such as time, requested address, response status and IP address) to run the service, find faults and prevent abuse.

Usage analytics (Mixpanel)

The app sends anonymous usage events to Mixpanel, hosted in the European Union. Examples are opening a pack page, adding a sound, or completing a purchase (with the product, price and currency). It also records basic usage such as how often the app is opened, when it was installed, and which campaign or source led to the install. Events are tied to a random device identifier created by Mixpanel, not to your name or email. Mixpanel may also receive the IP address of your connection.

Crash reports (Sentry)

If the app crashes or hits an error, a report is sent to Sentry. It can include the app and operating system version, device model, technical details of the error, the IP address of your connection and, when an error happens, a screenshot of the app screen in which sensitive content is masked.

Remote settings and notifications (Firebase)

The app uses Firebase Remote Config to fetch feature settings (for example whether the Shop is open). If you allow notifications, it also registers a push token with Firebase Cloud Messaging so I can send you occasional notices. You can turn notifications off in your device settings.

Ad measurement (Meta and TikTok)

To measure how well my ads work, versions of the app may use the Meta and TikTok SDKs. When they run, they can receive your device advertising identifier and events such as app install and launch, finishing the first sounds, viewing the paywall, starting a trial and purchases (with their value). They use this to tell me which ad led to an install or purchase.

  • If you are in the EU, the UK or Switzerland (worked out from your device time zone), these SDKs stay off until you tap Allow in the consent dialog. On iOS you also get Apple's tracking permission prompt.
  • Elsewhere, they start with the app. On iOS they still depend on Apple's tracking permission.
  • You can change your choice at any time in Menu > Privacy. Withdrawing consent stops collection and event forwarding right away. The TikTok SDK stops from the next launch.
  • On Android, the app also reads the Google Play install referrer once, to learn which campaign sent you to the store. This contains the campaign name, not personal data.
